# Voice craft and process

The no-tells rules stay in `SKILL.md` and `ai-tells.md`. This file is the method.

## Source order

1. The owner's exact request and the effect they want.
2. Their approved samples (in `VOICE.md`) and the current audience.
3. Project facts, brand decisions and required disclosures.
4. The craft rules.

Study supplied examples for what each gives you: rhythm, humor, tension, warmth, directness, specificity or restraint. Do not swap them for a generic idea of plain writing.

## The voice

- Human, warm, direct, specific. Name the house, the savings, the appointment or the real next step.
- Take a position when the source supports one. If there is a catch, say it in one plain line.
- Mix sentence length. Short punches. Longer sentences that finish a thought. A fragment or question when it fits. Uniform medium sentences are the biggest statistical tell.
- Say the word a person says out loud: "use", "buy", "help", not "utilize", "purchase", "facilitate".
- Contractions where natural. Active voice. And or But to start a sentence when it reads like talk.
- Adult language matched to the reader. Short must not strip out intelligence, feeling or a needed detail.
- High energy: strong verbs, real numbers, sure of itself.

## Paint the picture

When the facts support it, let the reader see the moment, the choice or the cost. A concrete scene beats an abstract benefit. Use a story when sequence makes the point. Use an analogy to connect something new to something the reader knows, and look outside the owner's own industry for it. Their industry is not the default analogy for unrelated copy. One clean image beats three. Do not force a story into every piece, stack metaphors, imitate a famous writer, or invent a personal detail for color.

## Questions

A tool, not a habit: one per short piece, two in a long one, never stacked, never with an obvious answer.

## Business terms

Exact business terms (lead, prospect, pitch, follow-up) stay when they are the right word. Swap only when vague, stiff or wrong for the audience.

## Process

1. Read the source, audience, context and examples. Note the format and the one action the copy drives.
2. Separate fixed facts and disclosures from language you can reshape.
3. Re-say the whole piece the way the owner would say it. Do not line-edit the original.
4. Compare with the samples in `VOICE.md`. Restore anything that went generic, too neat or flat.
5. Read it aloud. Re-say any sentence that sounds like a press release.
6. Hand back usable copy. Add a short note only for a material assumption, a missing fact, or a creative choice that affects shipping. If the owner has a gate on certain copy (see `PROFILE.md`), say it still needs their check.
