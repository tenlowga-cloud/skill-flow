# Where to look for a meeting brief

Read TOOLS.md to see which tools are connected. Go in this order. Stop early if you have enough. Note any tool you could not use under NOT CHECKED in the brief.

## 1. Calendar (always first)
- Open the event. Take: title, time, place or link, length, organizer.
- Attendees: names, emails, company domains. Mark who is outside the owner's team (team list is in PROFILE.md).
- Description: the agenda, the goal, any links.
- Attached docs: read titles; open only ones that look relevant.
- Past events with the same attendee: dates are your timeline.

## 2. CRM
- Find the contact by email first, then by name.
- Pull: company, role, how they came in (source field), deals (stage, value, dates), notes, tasks, last activity.
- Deals: stage, value, expected close, renewal date. Report exactly what the record says.
- Check for open tasks assigned to the owner. Those are "we promised".

## 3. Email
- Search threads with the attendee's address, then their domain, in the last 90 days.
- Read the newest 3 threads in full. Skim the rest by subject.
- Look for: promises made, questions unanswered, price talk, complaints, change of contact.
- Quote short phrases that show what they care about.

## 4. Notes or vault
- Search the person's full name, then company name, then email.
- Take the latest 2 notes. Check for mention of family or personal details; use only if the owner wrote it to be remembered (like "kid's name") and it is useful for rapport.

## 5. Transcripts and recorders
- Find the last call or meeting with them. Read the summary first, then search the transcript for: "need", "worried", "budget", "by when", "next step".
- Pull their own words, with the date.

## 6. Chat
- Search team chat threads that name them or their company. Look for internal context ("watch out, they are slow to pay").
- Do not quote internal gossip. Use it only as a flag, with the source.

## 7. Public
- Only if TOOLS.md allows web access.
- Their company website: what they sell, size, recent news.
- Their professional profile page if public: role, how long in role.
- Mark these [public] so the owner knows they are not first-hand.

## What to skip

- Anything marked private, confidential, or personal in a label, folder, or title.
- Health details, legal matters, money troubles, family problems, unless the owner needs them for this exact meeting and wrote them down for that. When in doubt, leave out and add a line: "Sensitive material exists in <source>, left out."
- Other clients' information found in the same thread or note.
- Passwords, tokens, account or ID numbers. Name the source file only.

## Two people with the same name

1. Match on email address first. An email is the strongest key.
2. Then company or domain, then city, then the role in the calendar invite.
3. If still unsure, show both matches in the brief under WHO, state which one you used and why, and mark it "please confirm".
4. Never merge facts from the two.

## If nothing is found

Say "No history found. First contact?" with the sources you searched. Offer a short list of questions to learn who they are. Do not fill space with guesses.
