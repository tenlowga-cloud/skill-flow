## Awareness

Eugene Schwartz: the frame depends on how much the prospect already knows. Most copy fails because it pitches the product to someone who doesn't yet feel the problem.

| Level | They know | Lead with | Example opener |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Nothing is wrong | A story, a surprising fact, a mirror of their life | "Most small shops lose a third of their calls after 5pm." (source the stat) |
| Problem aware | Something hurts, not the fix | Name the pain in their words, then show it's solvable | "You've been meaning to fix the website for two years." |
| Solution aware | The kind of fix exists | Why this approach beats the others | "A booking widget fills your calendar overnight, without hiring a receptionist." |
| Product aware | You exist | Proof, the offer, the risk removed | "Here's exactly what it costs for a three-truck shop." |
| Most aware | They want it | The ask, the deal, the date | "Your quote holds until Friday. Tonight or tomorrow better to finish the app?" |

Cold outreach is almost always unaware or problem aware. Follow-ups after a call are product or most aware. Match it.

## Frames

Pick one. Don't stack frames.

- **StoryBrand (Donald Miller).** A character (the prospect) has a problem (external, internal, philosophical), meets a guide (you: empathy plus authority) who gives them a plan and calls them to action, which helps them avoid failure and ends in success. Best for websites, decks, brand messages. The test: is the reader the hero and you the guide?
- **PAS: Problem, Agitate, Solve.** Name it, make them feel the cost honestly, offer the way out. Best for short emails, ads, texts. Agitate with truth, never fear-mongering.
- **Before, After, Bridge.** Here's life now, here's life after, here's how to get there. Best for problem-aware readers and testimonials.
- **Gap (Keenan, Gap Selling).** Current state (facts plus feelings), future state (facts plus feelings), the gap is the value, and the size of the gap sets the urgency. Best for discovery calls, proposals and reviews. Never pitch before the gap is clear to the prospect, in their words.
- **SPIN (Rackham)** for discovery: Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff questions. Implication questions ("What happens to this quarter if that system goes down for a week?") create urgency the prospect owns. Need-payoff questions ("How would it feel to know that's handled?") let them sell themselves. Use few situation questions: look facts up before the call.

## Proof placement

Proof goes right after the claim it supports, not in a testimonials block at the bottom. Order of strength:

1. A specific result for someone like them (named with permission, or described precisely).
2. A number with a source.
3. A demonstration (the calculator, the sample, the screenshot).
4. Relevant authority (license, years, specialty) stated once, plainly.
5. Volume ("200 families in Denver") only if true and countable.

Never invent proof. No proof yet: lean on a demonstration, a guarantee you can actually honor, or the reasoning itself.

## Objections

Answer the top objection before they raise it, inside the copy, in one or two lines. Common ones and the move:

| Objection | Move |
|---|---|
| "It costs too much" | Smallest honest unit, compare to a familiar spend, show the cost of not acting. When buyers tend to overestimate the price, put one honest real example right after the gap line. No real price yet: say it depends on their details and offer the real number, never a made-up range. |
| "I need to think about it" | Name it: "Most people want to sleep on it. Here's what to think about." Give the one question to decide on. |
| "I already have something" | Ask, don't argue: "When did you last check it still fits how you work now?" Offer a review, not a replacement. |
| "I don't trust salespeople" | Accusation audit plus a no-pressure ask. Make leaving easy. |
| "Not now" | Tie timing to a true trigger (a price change date, a renewal, an enrollment window). No trigger, set the next touch, don't push. |

## The close ladder

The close is the final yes. Most pieces can't win it in one step, so each piece wins the next rung and sets up the one after. Pick this piece's rung from where the prospect is now, not from where you wish they were.

| Rung | Typical asks | Usually won by |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Attention | Open, watch, stop scrolling | Ads, subject lines, hooks |
| 2. Conversation | Reply, answer a question, comment, DM | Cold email, text, DM, social post |
| 3. Commitment of time | Book a call, visit, demo, inspection, consult | Follow-ups, landing pages, voicemail |
| 4. Information | Fill the form, share the dec page, answer intake | Discovery calls, forms |
| 5. Evaluation | See the quote, proposal, trial, sample | Quote docs, proposals, trials |
| 6. Close | Sign, pay, apply, order, donate | Close calls, proposal follow-ups, checkout pages |
| 7. Expansion | Refer, review, renew, add on, buy again | Post-sale follow-ups, reviews, annual check-ins |

Rules:

- **Name the close first, then the rung.** "Close: signed annuity app. This piece: book the review." Every beat in the piece serves that rung.
- **One rung per piece.** Asking for two rungs at once ("book a call or just buy now") weakens both.
- **Skip rungs only when trust allows.** A warm, most-aware prospect can jump from a quote straight to signing. A cold one can't.
- **Every rung points to the next.** The confirmation, the thank-you screen and the follow-up all name the next step toward the close.
- **Measure the rung and the close.** A booking that never closes is a leak further down the ladder. Report both numbers when you have them.

## The ask

- One ask. Two asks halve the response to both.
- Size it to the trust earned. Cold: reply, a 10-minute call, a free check. Warm: a meeting, a quote. Hot: sign, buy.
- Say exactly what happens next and how long it takes. In one-to-one notes, ask a question they can answer instead of a reply instruction: "Is Tuesday or Thursday better for 15 minutes?"
- Give the true reason to act now, or none. Never invent a deadline.
- Make the default the right choice when you control a form or a page (pre-selected time slots, pre-filled fields).
- Commitment ladder: a tiny yes now makes the bigger yes later more likely. Ask for the smallest step that moves the deal forward.

## Live conversation

Calls, voicemails, video meetings. Chris Voss (*Never Split the Difference*) plus the gap.

- **Open with permission and a reason.** "I'll take 30 seconds to say why I called, then you decide if we keep talking. Fair?"
- **Tactical empathy.** Label feelings ("It sounds like..."), mirror the last 1 to 3 words as a question to get them talking, then stay quiet.
- **Calibrated questions** start with "how" or "what": "What would have to be true for this to make sense?" "How are you handling that today?"
- **Talk less.** The prospect should talk at least 60% of a discovery call. No monologue over 30 seconds.
- **Summarize to get "that's right".** Repeat their situation back so well they say "that's right". That's buy-in. "You're right" is a brush-off.
- **Voice.** The calm late-night radio voice for hard moments, a warm upbeat voice for most of the call. Slow down at the number and the ask.
- **Voicemail.** Under 25 seconds. Name, one specific reason tied to them, one easy next step, the number twice, slowly.

## Memory

- **Peak-end rule.** People judge an experience by its best moment and its last. Engineer one high point (the "aha" number, the relief) and end strong (a clear next step, a warm close).
- **Serial position.** Hook first, ask last. Bury nothing important in the middle.
- **Rule of three** for lists the reader must remember. Never more than three options on a decision (Hick's law: more choices, slower and fewer decisions).
