# Format playbooks

The spine (Stop, Feel, Trust, Ease, Act) in each format. Lengths are targets, not laws: shorter usually wins.

## Contents
- Cold email
- Follow-up email (after a call or quote)
- Text message (SMS)
- Follow-up sequence
- Call opener and script
- Voicemail
- Landing page or website section
- Ad (social or search)
- Flier, postcard, one-pager
- Deck or presentation
- Proposal or quote document
- Social post or DM
- Form, button, microcopy

## Cold email

- **Subject:** 2 to 5 words, lowercase reads personal, specific to them, no clickbait the body doesn't pay off. "your 2019 roof", "quick idea for the shop", "the approvals backlog".
- **Line 1:** about them, a real observation or trigger. Never your name or "I hope".
- **Lines 2 to 3:** the gap, in one or two sentences.
- **Line 4:** one line of proof or credibility.
- **Ask:** one small, easy question. "Worth a 10-minute look?" "Are you against a quick check?"
- 50 to 125 words. Plain text beats designed HTML for one-to-one outreach. No attachments, one link max.

## Follow-up email (after a call or quote)

- Subject names the thing: "your quote from Tuesday".
- Recap their situation in their words (shows you listened, gets the "that's right").
- The recommendation, one line on why it fits them.
- The number, plainly, smallest honest unit too.
- What happens next and how long it takes.
- One ask with a date or a true trigger.

## Text message

- Under 160 characters when possible. Name yourself on first contact. One ask.
- Easy answers: an either/or question ("Morning or afternoon better this week?"). Numbered reply codes only for automated broadcast texts.
- Respect quiet hours and the business's playbook hours. Opt-out language where the channel requires it.

## Follow-up sequence

- 4 to 7 touches over 2 to 4 weeks, mixed channels if allowed.
- Every touch adds something new: a proof point, a story, an answer to an objection, a useful tool. Never "just checking in".
- Last touch is the breakup: warm, says plainly what's ending, door left open ("This is the last time I'll bring this up. If you ever want me to look at what you've got..."). It often gets the most replies.

## Call opener and script

- Permission-based opener (see `structure.md#live-conversation`), then a calibrated question in the first 20 seconds.
- Discovery before any pitch: situation (few), problem, implication, need-payoff.
- Summarize to "that's right". Then the recommendation and one next step.
- Scripts are guardrails, not lines to read. Write them as beats plus key questions.

## Voicemail

Under 25 seconds. Name, one specific reason tied to them, the easy next step, the number twice slowly. Pair it with a text so they can reply without calling.

## Landing page or website section

Above the fold, in this order: a headline about their outcome, a subhead with the how or who it's for, one primary button with an outcome label, a risk-reducer under it, one trust signal (rating, credential, face).

Below: the gap (problem in their words), the plan (3 steps), proof next to claims, objections as a short FAQ, the same single ask repeated. One goal per page. Hand full site builds to the matching skill in `ROUTER.md` and run this as the persuasion pass.

## Ad

- The first 3 words or first second of video decide it. Hook on the reader's situation or a pattern break.
- One message, one audience, one action. The landing page must match the ad's promise word for word (message match).
- Faces, motion and contrast stop the scroll. Captions on video (most watch muted).
- Regulated categories (credit, housing, employment, insurance, health): check the platform's special ad category rules and make no guaranteed-outcome claims.

## Flier, postcard, one-pager

- Three-second test: headline, one image, one action, readable from arm's length.
- One offer. Big phone number or QR with a reason to scan ("Scan for your 2-minute quote").
- Front sells the outcome, back carries the proof and details.

## Deck or presentation

- One idea per slide, headline as a full-sentence takeaway.
- Story arc: their world, the problem, the cost, the plan, proof, the ask.
- The number slide gets the most white space. End on the ask, not "Questions?".

## Proposal or quote document

- Page 1: their goal in their words, the recommendation, the number, the next step. Most people read only page 1.
- Three options max, recommended one marked and in the middle.
- Anchor with the honest cost of the problem before the price.
- Never mix estimated and quoted numbers in one document. Where the business has its own quote process, follow it (the matching skill in `ROUTER.md`).

## Social post or DM

- Post: hook line that stops the scroll, one idea, a story or a useful fact, a soft ask (comment, DM a word).
- DM: personal, references something real about them, no pitch in the first message, ask a question.

## Form, button, microcopy

- Button: first-person outcome ("Get my quote").
- Field labels in plain words, examples in placeholders, errors that say how to fix it.
- Thank-you page: confirm, say exactly what happens next and when, give one small next action.
