# Worked examples

One per vertical, so the moves don't get stuck in one industry. Each shows the leak, the rebuild and the two test variants. Names and numbers are placeholders. Never reuse a placeholder number as a fact.

## 1. Cold email (B2B service, problem aware)

**Before**

> Subject: Marketing Solutions For Your Business
>
> Hi John, I hope this finds you well! My name is Sam and I wanted to reach out because our agency is an industry-leading provider of world-class digital marketing solutions. We have been in business for 12 years and we pride ourselves on our results. Act now, this is a limited time offer! Call us, visit our website, or reply to schedule a meeting.

**Leak:** it's about the agency, not John. No gap, fake urgency, four asks.

**Revamp**

> Subject: your missed calls after 5
>
> John, I called the shop at 6:15 last night and got voicemail. So did anyone else who searched "emergency plumber" then.

(Only if you actually made the call. A real test the reader can check beats any claim.)
>
> Most shops your size lose a few jobs a week that way. If yours is covered, I'll tell you in ten minutes and you're done.
>
> Is Tuesday or Thursday better for ten minutes?

**Variant A: hook.** Lead with the cost: "John, a missed 6pm call is usually a $400 job going to the next shop on Google." Bet: a dollar figure beats the anecdote for an owner who thinks in jobs. (Use a real average from your own numbers.)

**Variant B: proof.** Add one line: "[Shop in the next town] added [N] booked jobs a month by answering after hours." Bet: a nearby peer moves more than the logic. (Only if true and approved.)

| lever | what changed | why it moves them |
|---|---|---|
| You focus | "We" 9 times became "you" 5 times | The reader is the hero |
| Gap | A real test call showed the after-hours hole | Proof he can check himself |
| Ease | "If yours is covered, you're done" | Removes the fear of a pitch |
| Ask | Four asks became one either/or question | Easy to answer, no instruction |

## 2. Landing page hero (local trade, solution aware)

**Before**

> Welcome to Ridgeline Roofing. Quality Roofing Solutions Since 1998. We offer residential and commercial roofing, gutters, siding and more. [Learn More] [Contact Us] [Our Services]

**Leak:** a list of services with no outcome, three equal buttons, nothing to believe.

**Revamp**

> **Hail or leak damage? Know what you're dealing with this week.**
> Free inspection. Photo report you can send to your insurer.
> [Get my free inspection]
> 4.9 stars from [N] Front Range homeowners. [Call now] link above the photo on mobile.

**Variant A: frame.** "A roof you won't think about for 30 years." Bet: visitors who are replacing an old roof respond to the long-term outcome more than to the storm.

**Variant B: proof.** Swap the star line for one named local job with a photo. Bet: one neighbor's roof beats an aggregate rating.

Visual: one accent color on the button only, a real crew photo looking toward the headline, 60/30/10 split, button full width on phones. "This week" only if the schedule truly supports it.

## 3. Quote follow-up text (home services, most aware)

**Before**

> Hi just following up on the quote I sent over, let me know if you have any questions or want to move forward!

**Leak:** no reason, no number, no next step, hedged, and the next move is left to her.

**Revamp**

> Hi Maria, it's Dave from [company]. Your quote for the fence is $[X], and it includes hauling the old one away. Is this week or next better to get you on the schedule?

**Variant A: trigger.** Add the true reason: "Our spring schedule fills by [real date]." Bet: a real deadline lifts action.

**Variant B: ease.** "Want me to text the deposit link so you can lock the date from your phone?" Bet: naming the phone lowers effort more than a time choice.

## 4. Call opener (SaaS, cold, unaware)

**Before**

> Hi, this is Sam from ABC Software, how are you today? I'm calling because we have some great new features I'd love to tell you about.

**Revamp**

> Hi Dana, it's Sam with [company]. You weren't expecting my call, so I'll take 30 seconds to say why, and you tell me if it's worth another minute. Fair?
> (yes)
> I talk to a lot of ops leads at 20 to 50 person companies who still run approvals through email. Usually something gets stuck for a week and nobody notices until a customer does. How are you handling approvals right now?

**Variant A: label.** Open with an accusation audit: "This is a cold call, and you're probably about to hang up." Bet: naming it disarms faster.

**Variant B: question.** Swap the closing question for a no-oriented one: "Would it be crazy to spend 10 minutes seeing where yours get stuck?" Bet: lower pressure, more yeses.

## 5. Product page (e-commerce, solution aware)

**Before**

> Premium Ergonomic Office Chair. High quality materials. Adjustable. Free shipping on orders over $99. [Add to Cart]

**Leak:** features with no job, no proof, and nothing to remove the fear of buying a chair online.

**Revamp**

> **Sit through the 3pm slump without the back ache.**
> Adjusts to your height in 30 seconds. Try it for 30 nights. If it's not for you, we pick it up.
> [Add to cart: $[X]] Ships in [real window].
> ★ 4.7 from [N] reviews: "[short real review quote about back pain]"

**Variant A: proof.** Put a customer photo review above the fold. Bet: a real person in the chair beats studio shots.

**Variant B: price.** Show "$[X/30] a month with [real financing]" under the price. Bet: the smallest honest unit lowers sticker shock.

## 6. The flop pattern (a monthly "newsletter" drip, dental practice)

A pattern seen in real campaigns: a monthly drip to a bought or old list that announces itself, asks a quiz question and tells the reader it is not selling. Typical result on a dirty list: a few opens, almost no clicks, no replies. Treat any numbers like these as direction, not a clean test. The lesson applies in any vertical.

**Before**

> Subject: Your smile is covered. Is your cleaning schedule?
>
> Hi [FIRST NAME], Thanks for connecting with us! As promised, you'll receive one short, useful email each month, always focused on practical ways to keep your teeth healthy. Let's start with cleanings. Did you know that regular cleanings can prevent costly dental work? ... We're not here to sell, but to share what you should know. Curious about how it works? Just hit reply with any question. The team at [PRACTICE]

**Leak:** a team, not a person. It announces a newsletter instead of raising a situation. "Did you know" is a quiz, not a question they'd answer. "Not here to sell" flags the sale. The ask is a generic reply instruction.

**Revamp**

> Subject: when was your last cleaning?
>
> Hi [FIRST NAME], it's [NAME] at [PRACTICE].
>
> I was going through our schedule and noticed it has been a while since your last visit. If a tooth started hurting next week, would you know who to call, or would you be searching for an emergency appointment?
>
> -[NAME]

**Variant A: situation.** "If a filling cracked on a Saturday, what would you do?" Bet: a concrete scene raises the felt stake.

**Variant B: ask.** End on "Would a Tuesday or Thursday morning be easier for a 30-minute cleaning?" Bet: a bookable either/or beats an open question when the reader is already a patient.
