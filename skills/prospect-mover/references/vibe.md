# Vibe: how it feels before they read a word

Vibe is the snap judgment: do I trust this, do I like this, is this for someone like me. It forms in under a second and colors everything after.

## 1. Warmth first, competence second

Susan Fiske's research on social judgment: people sort others on warmth (are you on my side?) and competence (can you do it?). Warmth is judged first and weighs more. A competent sender who feels cold reads as a threat. A warm sender who feels incompetent reads as pity. You need both, in that order.

- **Warmth signals:** their name, their situation, a real detail from the last conversation, plain language, a small kindness with no strings.
- **Competence signals:** one specific credential that matters to this buyer, a precise number, a clear plan, clean formatting, no typos.

## 2. Status and calm confidence

- **Fewer words read as higher status.** Over-explaining reads as needing the deal.
- **No begging language.** Remove "I'd really appreciate", "if you get a chance", "sorry to bother you".
- **Make leaving easy.** "If now's not the time, no problem, I'll check back in the spring." Freedom to say no raises the odds of yes (the "but you are free" effect).
- **Calm beats hype.** Exclamation points, all caps and urgency words cheapen the sender. One exclamation point per piece at most, zero in anything about money or risk.

## 3. Likability (Cialdini's liking)

People say yes to people they like. Liking comes from:

- **Similarity:** shared place, background, kids, sport, profession. Only real ones.
- **Genuine compliments:** specific and true ("Your shop has the best reviews on that street").
- **Cooperation:** "us vs the problem", not "me vs you".
- **Familiarity:** repeated, consistent, low-pressure presence (mere-exposure effect). This is why a steady follow-up sequence works.

## 4. Match the room

- Read the prospect's register and meet it one step more polished. A contractor texting "yo" gets a short, friendly text, not a formal letter. A CFO gets numbers first. A first-time founder gets plain words and one next step.
- Mirror their words and pace. Formal in, formal out. Short in, short out.
- Pick one emotion for the piece: relief, pride, belonging, protection, momentum. Every word supports it.

## 5. Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone |
|---|---|
| Cold first touch | Curious, light, specific, zero pressure |
| After a call | Warm, precise, "here's what we said, here's the next step" |
| Price or quote | Calm, plain, confident, numbers stated without apology |
| Bad news or a hard topic (a failure, a loss, money gaps, health, death) | Slow, gentle, direct. Name it once, then move to the plan |
| Re-engaging a cold lead | Friendly, easy out, a new reason to talk |
| Referral ask | Grateful, specific about who you help best |

## 6. Vibe killers

- Sender-centered openers, long intros, corporate filler.
- Template smell: `{FirstName}` errors, generic praise, obvious mail merge.
- Fake friendliness ("Hey friend!!") from a stranger.
- Fear-mongering about loss, failure, illness or money beyond what's true and needed.
- Visual clutter (see `visual.md`) that says "cheap" before a word is read.
