# Vertical lens: a worked example (insurance)

This is one finished vertical lens, written for insurance and financial-product sales. It exists to show the shape. Copy `lens-template.md` and build your own for your vertical. Do not apply this file to any other vertical. It carries no carrier, product or person names on purpose; fill those from your own facts.

Load only for insurance and financial-product work, together with `verticals.md#regulated-work`. The universal call moves are in `call-playbook.md`.

## What a call scoreboard looks like

Keep a short table of your real calls and what decided each one. Patterns from a set of reviewed calls:

| Call type | Outcome | What decided it |
|---|---|---|
| Annuity follow-up (couple) | Signing booked | The blocker was cleared out loud, then one specific time slot was offered |
| Life, existing client, no budget | Booked, application pending | A short story plus two money-saving tips first, price framed yearly, annual pay offered |
| Term quote, four options | Soft yes, no date | "Which option sounds best right now?" got a pick, but no application started and no date set |
| Retention review | Callback booked | The gap was tied to a life event, with a specific time: "tomorrow about 12:30, sound good?" |
| Discovery, term versus permanent (retirees) | Numbers call booked | The "anything else I should consider?" sweep surfaced the real question |
| Rollover, bonus product | Follow-up booked, test amount | A big anchor was scaled to a small test, plus the humor veto |
| Underwriting results, best rate class | Stalled warm | Good-news lead, but two different "original" prices in one call and no ask after the need was named |
| Policy review, large life upsell | Stalled: "send quotes" | Pitched with no size and no price |
| Cash-value pitch, young investor | Lost: email dodge | No gap found, heavy stories up front, the pitch ended on "Right. So,", and "email me" was accepted with no time attached |

## Moves that worked

- The cross-sell rides a service task: "Did they talk to you about an umbrella?" "Do you have a renters policy?"
- Rent versus own for term and permanent coverage, with the real date cliff: "A 10-year term ends at 80 and can't be renewed."
- Living benefits make the policy useful while you are alive, not only at death. Use the carrier's exact rider language.
- Price framed yearly ("under three hundred a year") and annual pay for clients who dislike monthly bills.
- "Pre-indicated, no exam" as a risk remover, only when the carrier actually offers it.
- Annual reviews as the door: few convert right away, but they plant seeds and surface umbrellas, scheduled items, rentals and business policies.

## Fact-finding specifics

- **DIME** to size life needs: debts, income (a multiple of household income for a stated number of years), mortgage, education, minus existing coverage. People who guess a round number usually need several times that.
- "How much life insurance do you have in place?" not "Do you have any?"
- "Do you remember why you bought it? What problem were you solving? Is that still a problem?"
- "Return **on** your money or return **of** your money?"
- Life covers "if you never show up again". Disability covers "if you can't show up for a while".
- The wall analogy for asking about assets: the policy is a wall between what you have built and the bad days. The more you have built, the stronger the wall needs to be.

## Say it this way instead (compliance-safe rewrites)

| Said on a call | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Mini disability option built in" | "It has living-benefit riders: if you're diagnosed with a qualifying chronic, critical or terminal illness, you can access part of the death benefit early. It isn't disability insurance." (Use the carrier's own rider wording and percentages.) |
| "They don't ask questions, they write a check" | "Once a doctor certifies the condition under the rider's terms, you can take part of the benefit early. It reduces the death benefit." |
| "No risk of losing money" (indexed annuity) | "Index losses don't come out of your account. Surrender charges and fees still apply." |
| "31% bonus day one" | "This product has a [X]% premium bonus, with [vesting or surrender] terms. Here's the illustration." |
| "Averaging nine to eleven percent" | "Illustrated at [X]%, not guaranteed. The guaranteed column is on page [N]." |
| "90% liquid day two" | Quote the contract's exact access terms on the day of signing. |
| "Pre-approved" | "Pre-indicated" only if the carrier uses it, and say what still has to happen before approval. |
| "You just lit that money on fire" (term at older ages) | "A 10-year term ends at 80 and can't be renewed, so here's what that costs you compared to coverage that lasts." |
| "Act dumb" (a common training tip) | Be genuinely curious and ask simple questions. Never hide what you know about a product or fee. |
| Anchor at the maximum and adjust down | Start at the amount the needs math supports and the client says they can afford, and document why. |
| A small cash bet on the needs calculator | Don't use it. Cash inducements can break rebating and advertising rules. |

## Regulatory rules that change the copy

- **No promised outcomes.** No "guaranteed" unless the product guarantees it, and then say exactly what is guaranteed and by whom. No "you will earn", "can't lose", "risk-free" for products with risk, fees or surrender charges.
- **Index floors are not "can't lose".** Fees, surrender charges and caps still apply. Write: "Index losses don't come out of your account. The rider fee still does, every year, even a flat one." Name the costs before the reader finds them.
- **No manufactured decision dates.** A rollover lead time or rate change is a real date only if you verified it. Never leave a bracketed deadline for someone to fill in later.
- **Illustrations and projections** are labeled hypothetical, not guaranteed, and shown with their assumptions. Never pull a projected number into a headline as if it were a promise.
- **Disclosures stay beside the claim** they qualify, readable, not buried in a footer.
- **Fear stays factual.** No scare tactics, no exaggerated odds, no exploiting grief. Older buyers of annuities and long-term care get extra care: clear, slow, easy to involve family.
- **Suitability and best interest.** Recommendations follow the suitability and best-interest rules in your state. Copy can't push a product as right for everyone.
- **No disparaging or misleading comparisons.** Replacing an existing policy has its own disclosure process.
- **Testimonials** need documentation, and for financial products may carry extra rules. None without the owner's approval.
- **Licensing.** Outreach identifies the agent or agency as required, and only for products and states where the agent is licensed.
- **Channel rules.** Before outreach to a purchased list, check the source disclosed this kind of follow-up. Texts and calls follow consent and quiet-hours rules. Email needs an unsubscribe. Social platforms may treat insurance and credit ads as special categories.
- A licensed human runs the final check on every client-facing piece.

## Persuasion that stays safe and strong here

- The cost of the gap, in true numbers for their situation.
- Status quo bias: "the plan you set up years ago was built for a different mortgage".
- A quick, free review (reciprocity plus low effort).
- Real triggers: a birthday rate change, a new baby, a new house, a renewal.
- Real, similar social proof: "Most families I sit down with find one gap."

## Examples for the core files

- Loss frame: "Don't leave your family $400,000 short" beats "get $400,000 of coverage."
- Concrete: "The mortgage gets paid off" beats "financial security for your family."
- Implication question: "What happens to the house payment if your income stops?"
- Objection "I already have something": "When did you last check it covers today's mortgage?" Offer a review, not a replacement.
- Price: people badly overestimate what term life costs. When a real quote exists, put one honest example (age, amount, term, price) right after the gap line.
- Older readers: use high contrast and 18px body text.
